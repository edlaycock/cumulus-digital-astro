The phone goes quiet for almost every trade in Surrey around now, and every November we have the same conversation with worried builders, plumbers and electricians. The work has not disappeared. It has moved into living rooms, where homeowners in Walton, Weybridge and Guildford are planning what gets done in the New Year and deciding, quietly, who they will call.

This article is about making sure that call comes to you.

## Why does work dry up for trades in November and December?

Because homeowners stop booking and start planning. Nobody wants a kitchen ripped out two weeks before the in-laws arrive, so non-urgent jobs get parked. But parked is not cancelled. The loft conversion, the rewire, the new driveway: they all move to a mental list marked January.

What most trades miss is that the research still happens now. People sit down in the quiet evenings, search for the job they want done, read reviews, compare websites and build a shortlist. By the time they ring anyone, the real decision is mostly made. A quiet phone in November does not mean a quiet market. It means the market has gone silent on you while it reads.

That changes what November is for. It is not the month to win calls. It is the month to win shortlists.

## What should I do in November to win January jobs?

Make yourself the easiest trade in your town to research. Everything else follows from that.

Start with your Google Business Profile, because it is where nearly every local search ends up. Add photos from jobs you finished this autumn, with the town in the description. Check every service you offer is actually listed. Then ask your last five or six happy customers for a review while the job is still fresh in their mind. A profile with recent photos and reviews from October beats one that has sat untouched since spring, both in the map results and in what AI assistants say about you.

Next, look at your website the way a stranger would. Does it say which towns you cover? Does each service you offer have its own page, or is everything squeezed onto one? Does it answer the questions you get asked on every quote visit, like how long the job takes and what affects the price? We wrote about why those question pages matter so much in [our guide to getting recommended by ChatGPT](/how-to-get-cited-by-ai/), and the short version is that the same pages that persuade a homeowner also feed Google and AI answers.

Finally, put something new out. One honest article or even a photo post about a recent job tells Google, and every person researching you, that the business is alive and busy. Silence reads as closed.

## How do homeowners in Surrey choose a trade they have never used?

They triangulate. A recommendation in a local Facebook group gets checked against your Google reviews, which get checked against your website. Any weak link breaks the chain. A glowing mention in the Walton group leads to a profile with three reviews and no photos, and the homeowner quietly moves to the next name.

This is worth taking seriously because Surrey homeowners have plenty of choice and high expectations. The trades that stay booked through winter are rarely the cheapest. They are the ones whose evidence lines up everywhere a nervous customer looks. Real photos, named towns, a website that answers questions plainly, reviews that mention the actual work. None of it is complicated. Most of your competitors still have not done it, which is exactly the opportunity.

## Does a quiet season make marketing a waste of money?

The opposite, and this is the mistake we see most. Trades cut marketing in December because the phone is quiet, then wonder why February is quiet too. There is a lag built into how this industry buys. The enquiries you get in January come from visibility you built in November and December. Cut the engine now and you coast for a while, then stop.

If money is tight, cut selectively. Advertising aimed at urgent, same-week work can wind down, because nobody books a patio in December. Keep anything that builds the shortlist: review requests, your Business Profile, content, and the website itself. If the site is the weak link, the quiet weeks are honestly the best time of the year to fix it, because you can afford the disruption now and you cannot in March. That is most of the work we do for trades, and you can see how we approach it on [our trades and construction page](/services/trades/).

## What does a good January pipeline look like by Christmas?

By the last week of December you want three things true. Your Google profile shows this autumn's work and this autumn's reviews. Your website has a page for each main service, answering real questions, naming the towns you cover. And at least a few homeowners have already emailed or messaged about New Year work, because planners enquire early even when they book late.

Hit those three and January tends to take care of itself. The phone that went quiet in November rings with people who feel like they already know you, which are the easiest jobs to quote and the best customers to have.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why has my phone stopped ringing in November?

Homeowners stop booking non-urgent work in the run-up to Christmas and start planning instead. They are still researching, reading reviews and shortlisting, but the calls come in January. If your website and Google profile are not working hard in November, you fall off the January shortlist before it is even written down.

### When do homeowners actually book January work?

Most of the deciding happens between mid November and the New Year, quietly, on sofas and in group chats. The booking call comes the first full week of January. The trades who win that call are the ones the household already found, read about and half chose over Christmas.

### What is the single best thing a Surrey trade can do this month?

Update your Google Business Profile properly: current photos of recent jobs, services listed in full, a handful of fresh reviews requested from this autumn's customers, and accurate hours. It is free, it takes an evening, and it is the first thing both Google and ChatGPT look at when someone asks who to use.

### Should I pause my marketing over Christmas to save money?

Pausing in December is how you end up quiet in February. The research that fills January diaries happens during the quiet weeks, so December marketing is buying next quarter's work. If budget is tight, cut spend on ads for emergency callouts, keep the review requests and the content going.
